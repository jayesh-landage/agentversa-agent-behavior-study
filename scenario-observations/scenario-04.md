## 6. Feasible Options

### Option A: Limited Administrative Session Today

The panel could use today's available time only for procedural and administrative matters, without taking substantive testimony.

Potential topics could include explaining the proposed process, confirming participant availability, identifying accessibility and interpretation arrangements, and organizing written submissions.

**Advantages:**
- Makes limited use of today's available resources.
- May help clarify the remaining procedural requirements.
- Could support preparation for a later substantive session.

**Limitations:**
- The inaccessible room remains a barrier for the affected service user.
- The representative's remote connection limits detailed exhibit review.
- W1's interpreter is not available before 10:45.
- The panel must ensure that administrative discussion does not become substantive testimony without the required arrangements.

### Option B: Postpone to the Accessible Room Tomorrow

The panel could postpone the session to the accessible room available the following day.

**Advantages:**
- Addresses the known step-free access requirement.
- Provides an opportunity to coordinate substantive testimony with the qualified interpreter.
- Allows time to confirm participant availability and support for written submissions.

**Limitations:**
- The supplied packet does not confirm that the representative, interpreter, and all other required participants can attend at the same time tomorrow.
- Postponement delays the inquiry's next procedural step.
- Assistance for preparing written submissions still needs to be arranged.
- The intake backlog and urgent service-user needs remain separate operational concerns.

Before scheduling substantive testimony, the panel should confirm the interpreter's availability and the representative's participation arrangements for the new session.

### Option C: Staged Administrative and Substantive Sessions

The panel could conduct a limited administrative session today, if fair participation is possible, and reserve substantive testimony for a later session in the accessible room with the required interpreter and confirmed participant availability.

**Advantages:**
- Separates procedural preparation from substantive testimony.
- May reduce avoidable delay while protecting meaningful participation.
- Allows the panel to identify unresolved scheduling and accessibility requirements before the substantive session.

**Limitations:**
- Requires clear communication about which matters can be addressed today.
- Requires confirmation of the later session's arrangements.
- Some participants may need help preparing written submissions.
- The panel must ensure that no substantive evidence is considered in a way that unfairly disadvantages an absent participant.

This option requires a clear boundary between administrative preparation and substantive matters.
