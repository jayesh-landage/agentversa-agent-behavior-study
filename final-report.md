# Final Report — NyayaSetu Agent Behavior Study

## Report Status

This is a working report based on the scenario information currently available. It should not be treated as a final evidence-complete report until the individual NyayaSetu responses for Scenarios 1 and 2 have been added and analyzed.

## 1. Research Question

To what extent does NyayaSetu's observed behavior align with its intended court-administration role when it encounters incomplete information, uncertainty, competing priorities, and coordination requirements?

## 2. Agent and Role Design

NyayaSetu is described as a structured Court Administration Agent designed to support efficient and reliable court operations.

The original submitted design is intended to serve as the baseline for evaluating the agent. The provenance of the current Version 1 document must be recorded accurately, including whether it reproduces, summarizes, or expands the original submission.

## 3. Method and Evidence

The study separates the initial design, predictions, scenario observations, cross-scenario findings, and proposed design changes.

Claims about individual agent behavior should be supported by identifiable episode records. Collective simulation summaries are treated as group-level evidence rather than substitutes for individual agent responses.

The analysis distinguishes recorded facts from interpretations, alternative explanations, and unresolved questions.

## 4. Scenario Findings

### Scenario 0 — Roles, Boundaries, and Coordination

The available Decision Timeline summary records NyayaSetu's proposed action as identifying information needs for effective court operations.

This is consistent with the agent's high-level administrative role. Because the scenario provided no substantive case facts or evidence, the finding is limited to a baseline observation.

No separate pre-result prediction was recorded.

### Scenario 1 — The Interrupted Service

The portal outage lasted 47 minutes. K17 authentication occurred at 09:08, before the outage began at 09:12.

The temporal sequence does not establish causation, identify the person who performed the account activity, or prove misconduct. Four users expressed urgent needs within the next 24 hours, and the incident report did not definitively establish whether assistance decisions were irrevocably lost.

The individual NyayaSetu response and identifiable episode reference are not included in the available material. Therefore, the agent-specific decision and its quality cannot yet be assessed reliably.

### Scenario 2 — The Two Clocks

The packet includes an exported server log, a collection note, a clock note, a building-access excerpt, and a cropped screenshot.

The records have important limitations. The server clock was found six minutes slow at noon, but the onset and consistency of drift are unknown. Matching checksums support transfer integrity of the collected copy but do not establish that the source interpretation is correct. The staging-folder access history is undocumented for an 18-minute interval. The building-access record does not establish who held the badge, and the screenshot's provenance is undocumented.

The collective simulation report states that agents generally recommended retrieving an independent authentication source. It also states that the resource trade-off was acknowledged and no external action was recorded as completed.

The individual NyayaSetu response is not included in the available material, so the collective recommendation cannot be attributed to NyayaSetu as an individual decision.

## 5. Findings Currently Supported

The available material supports the following limited findings:

1. The Scenario 0 summary entry is consistent with NyayaSetu's high-level administrative role.
2. Scenario 1 requires a distinction between chronology, causation, account activity, and personal identity.
3. Scenario 2 contains substantial uncertainty in timestamp interpretation and evidence provenance.
4. The Scenario 2 collective report describes a general recommendation to retrieve an independent authentication source.
5. Individual agent records are necessary to assess NyayaSetu's actual decision-making.

## 6. Unexpected Behavior and Failure Modes

Potential risks to evaluate include overconfidence in timestamps, treating account activity as identity evidence, interpreting missing access records as proof of alteration, overlooking urgent operational needs, and presenting proposed actions as completed.

These are possible failure modes to investigate, not established NyayaSetu errors.

## 7. Cooperation and Influence

The collective Scenario 2 report describes broad agreement among agents. It does not establish whether NyayaSetu independently reached the same conclusion, influenced another agent, or followed another agent's recommendation.

Individual episode records are required for a traceable assessment of cooperation and influence.

## 8. Version 2 Proposal

Possible future tests include explicit evidence-status labels, structured comparison of constrained options, and clearer distinctions between proposed and completed actions.

These remain proposals. Their value must be assessed through documented evidence and, where implemented, future testing.

## 9. Ethics and Limitations

The simulation is fictional and educational. Its agents cannot issue binding rulings or take real-world actions. Results may be affected by scenario design, prompts, model variability, available information, memory, interactions, and platform constraints.

A small number of simulated episodes cannot establish real-world legal competence or deployment readiness.

## 10. Conclusion

The study has documented a limited baseline observation and the evidence challenges presented by Scenarios 1 and 2.

The next priority is to retrieve and cite NyayaSetu's individual episode responses, analyze its actual decisions against the supplied evidence, and update the findings accordingly.

Until those records are available, claims about NyayaSetu's individual behavior and cross-scenario consistency must remain provisional.
